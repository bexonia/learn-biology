✅ Decomposed 3D Subunits & Biophysical Architecture (7 Pieces)

Unlike a prokaryotic cell where transcription, translation, and metabolism occur in a single shared cytoplasmic compartment, the eukaryotic animal cell is a ~20 µm metropolis partitioned into specialized membrane-bound organelles. A cholesterol-stiffened plasma membrane encloses the cytosol, while the double-membraned Nucleus sequesters the linear chromosomal genome, the Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) and Golgi Apparatus form a continuous secretory assembly line, Mitochondria generate ATP via oxidative phosphorylation, Lysosomes and Peroxisomes handle acidic recycling and peroxide detoxification, and a three-polymer Cytoskeleton (actin, microtubules, intermediate filaments) provides mechanical architecture and motorized highway tracks.

✅ Decomposed 3D Subunits & Biophysical Architecture (6 Pieces)

While all eukaryotes share a membrane-bound nucleus, mitochondria, ER, Golgi, and 80S ribosomes, the three major multicellular/crown eukaryotic lineages evolved radically different outer envelopes and specialized organelles to match their lifestyles: (1) Animal cells lack a cell wall, relying on a flexible cholesterol-rich membrane tethered to an extracellular collagen/fibronectin matrix via integrins for motility and phagocytosis; (2) Plant cells encase themselves in a rigid crystalline Cellulose + Hemicellulose + Pectin cell wall pierced by cytoplasmic Plasmodesmata bridges, powered by photosynthetic Chloroplasts and inflated by a central Turgor Vacuole occupying up to 80% of cell volume; (3) Fungal cells build a tough cross-linked β-(1,3)/β-(1,6)-Glucan and Chitin cell wall over an Ergosterol-stiffened plasma membrane.

✅ Decomposed 3D Subunits & Biophysical Architecture (7 Pieces)

Animal cells weave an intricate Extracellular Matrix (ECM) dominated by Type I/IV Collagen triple helices (Gly-X-Y repeats with hydroxyproline), elastic Fibronectin dimers, and hydrated Proteoglycan glycosaminoglycan brushes. Across the plasma membrane, heterodimeric α/β Integrins act as two-way allosteric mechanical clutches: in their resting bent-closed state they have low affinity, but upon inside-out Talin binding (or outside-in binding to the Arg-Gly-Asp [RGD] loop of Fibronectin), the Integrin legs swing open by 130 Å. Inside the cell, tensile force unfolds α-helical bundles in Talin, exposing cryptic binding sites that recruit Vinculin, Paxillin, and Focal Adhesion Kinase (FAK) to lock onto contractile F-actin stress fibers.

📚 Curated Online & Structural Biology References

Eukaryotic proteostasis balances two giant barrel-shaped nanomachines that determine the life or death of every cytoplasmic protein: the **TRiC/CCT Chaperonin** (which folds complex cytoskeletal and WD40 proteins) and the **26S Proteasome** (which destroys misfolded or short-lived regulatory proteins).
