BIOPHYSICAL ROLE & MECHANISM

Cytosolic rotor turbine base and rotational direction switch. FliG interacts with MotA stators to convert proton influx into mechanical rotary torque (~4,600 pN·nm). BIOPHYSICAL ROLE & MECHANISM

The universal ribozyme engine of protein synthesis. It translates genetic information encoded in messenger RNA into amino acid polypeptides, consuming 2 GTP and 2 ATP per peptide bond with an elongation rate of ~15–20 amino acids per second.

Bacterial Flagellar Motor Cryo-EM Architecture

Biophysical Principles of Nature's Rotary Nanomotor

Energy Source: Driven by the Proton Motive Force (Δp = Δψ - 60 ΔpH ≈ -170 mV). Protons stream from the periplasm through 11 MotA/MotB stator channels into the cytoplasm, consuming ~1,200 protons per complete revolution.

Torque Generation: Electrostatic interactions between charged residues on MotA (Arg90, Glu98) and FliG (Arg281, Asp288, Glu289) drive power strokes delivering ~4,600 pN·nm of stall torque.

Directional Switching: When chemotaxis signaling protein CheY-P binds FliM/FliN, it flips FliG subunits ~180°, reversing rotation from CCW (smooth swimming) to CW (tumbling).
